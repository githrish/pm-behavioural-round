# Step 4: PM translation for people moving into PM

Interviewers for career switchers ask two silent questions: **"Have they already done PM work, even without the title?"** and **"Do they understand what a PM actually does?"** The stories must answer yes to both, honestly.

## The rule: translate the work, not the title

PM-adjacent work counts: finding user problems, shaping what gets built, deciding with data, aligning people around a plan. Find those parts of the user's story and lead with them. Never claim a PM title, roadmap ownership or decisions that someone else made. Say "I brought the problem to the PM, and here's how it changed the roadmap" rather than "I owned the roadmap".

## How to find the PM part of any story

Ask the user these questions about each story:
- Did you talk to customers and notice a pattern others missed?
- Did you take that pattern to product or engineering? What changed because of it?
- Did you choose between options or priorities? On what basis?
- Did you define or track a metric, run a test or compare versions?
- Did you get people from different teams to agree on something?
- Did you build or spec anything: a tool, a process, a dashboard, a workflow, an internal product?

## Translation table by function

| From | What they did (resume language) | What it is in PM terms | Example reframe |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sales / account management** | Closed deals, managed accounts, hit quota | Customer discovery, requirements, revenue impact of product gaps, influencing the roadmap | "Closed 40 enterprise deals" → "Ran discovery with 40 enterprise buyers, found that missing SSO was blocking 30% of deals, made the case to product, and it shipped in Q3, unblocking ₹1.2 Cr pipeline" |
| **GTM / product marketing** | Launches, positioning, pricing, enablement | Problem framing, segmentation, go-to-market strategy, launch execution, pricing experiments | "Led launch of X" → "Defined the target segment and value proposition for X, ran a pricing test across 2 tiers, and drove adoption to Y% in 60 days" |
| **Growth / performance marketing** | Campaigns, CAC, funnels, A/B tests | Funnel analysis, experimentation, metric ownership, hypothesis-driven work | "Ran campaigns" → "Diagnosed a 40% drop at signup step 2, tested 3 hypotheses, and lifted activation 12%" |
| **Customer success / support / ops** | Tickets, escalations, onboarding, SOPs | Voice of the customer, pain-point prioritisation, process/internal product design | "Handled escalations" → "Categorised 2,000 tickets, found 3 root causes, and worked with engineering to fix the top one, cutting tickets 25%" |
| **Engineering** | Built features, architecture, on-call | Technical trade-offs, scoping, delivery, user impact | Lead with *why* it was built and for whom, then the trade-offs, then impact; tech detail only as needed |
| **Design / UX research** | Research, flows, prototypes | User insight, problem definition, solution exploration | Show the business metric and prioritisation, not only the craft |
| **Data / analytics** | Dashboards, analyses, models | Metric definition, insight → decision, experimentation | Show the decision the analysis drove and the outcome, not the query |
| **Consulting** | Client recommendations, decks | Problem structuring, strategy, stakeholder alignment | Show what got *implemented* and its result, not just what was recommended |

## Language to use (only where true)

discovery, user problem, pain point, hypothesis, experiment, success metric, trade-off, prioritised, scoped, MVP, iterated, launched, adoption, retention, activation, funnel, roadmap input, cross-functional, aligned stakeholders.

## Language to drop or soften

quota, pipeline (keep only as the business result), "closed", "managed the account", campaign jargon, internal tool names nobody outside will know.

## The "Why PM?" bridge

Every switcher gets asked this, usually right after "Tell me about yourself". Write a 30 to 45 second answer with this structure:
1. **The pull:** the moment or pattern that made them want to own *what gets built*, not just sell, market or support it. Make it specific.
2. **The proof:** 1 or 2 things they've already done that are PM work (from the stories), plus any deliberate prep (side project, course, internal PM shadowing).
3. **The edge:** what their background gives them that many PMs lack (customer intimacy from sales, distribution sense from GTM, funnel instinct from growth).

Avoid: "I want to be the CEO of the product", "I'm tired of sales", or anything vague about "strategy".

## Gap check

Switchers' stories often miss **prioritisation** and **working with engineering**. If no top-3 story shows them, flag it and look for a smaller backup story in step 6, even an internal tool, a side project or a cross-team initiative.
