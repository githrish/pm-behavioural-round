# Step 3: Draft from material, then fill gaps (max 5 questions per story)

Don't interview from a blank page. Work on **one story at a time**:

1. **Draft first.** Pull everything the resume and the other material say about the story into a STAR++ draft. Mark each part as known, partly known (e.g. a number with no baseline) or missing.
2. **Check for conflicts.** If two sources disagree (a number, a date), that's one of your questions.
3. **Ask at most 5 questions, one per call of the question tool.** Only ask about gaps, in this priority order, and skip any the material already answers:
   1. **The call:** what options there were, what they chose, and what they gave up.
   2. **Their part:** what they owned versus influenced, and who they had to bring along.
   3. **Numbers:** before and after, and how it was measured.
   4. **The bump:** what went wrong partway through, and what they changed.
   5. **++:** what they learned, and what they do differently now.

   If the material covers most of these, ask fewer. Fold small confirmations, like "is it ₹10L or ₹1Cr?", into the same question as a related gap instead of spending a question on them.
4. **Write it in full and show it.** Let the user correct everything in one reply, using the review question in the question tool.

Usually that's 3 to 5 questions per story and about 15 for three stories. If a gap is still open after 5 questions, write the story without it and mark it as a gap rather than asking more.

## What interviewers score in a story

- **Ownership:** did you spot the problem, or were you handed it? Did you keep going when it got hard?
- **Judgment:** the call you made wasn't obvious, and you can say why you chose it over the alternatives. Jackie Bavaro calls this the "epiphany" and treats it as the clearest sign of seniority.
- **Your part:** "I" for what you did. Amazon's interview guide says the same.
- **Measured impact:** a number, tied to a business or user goal. "We launched" is not a result.
- **Scope fits the level:** a feature for a PM, a team's direction for a senior PM, cross-org work above that.
- **Self-awareness:** a real learning, and what you now do differently. These are the "++".

## Question bank (pick from here to fill the 5 slots; ask only what's missing)

- **Context:** What was going on? Why did it matter to the business then? What would have happened if nobody acted?
- **Your role:** What did you own, and what did you only influence? Who gave you this, or did you pick it up yourself?
- **Users and problem:** Who was the user? How did you know the problem was real: data, calls, tickets?
- **The call:** What options did you have? What did you choose, and what did you give up? What did you see that others didn't?
- **People:** Who disagreed, and why? How did you bring them round?
- **Setbacks:** What went wrong partway through? What did you change?
- **Results:** What was the before and after, as a number? How did you measure it? What happened later?
- **Learning:** What do you know now that you didn't then?
- **Now:** What habit did this change?

Record every raw detail in story-bank.md. Most of it won't fit in 2 minutes, but it's what you'll need for follow-up questions.

## Write it in STAR++

| Part | What goes here | Share of time |
|---|---|---|
| **Headline** | One line with the result and your role, said first | ~5% |
| **Situation** | The stakes, in 1 or 2 sentences. Make the interviewer care about the user or the business. | ~10% |
| **Task** | What you were responsible for and the goal | ~5% |
| **Action** | 3 things you did. One must be **the call you made and what you gave up**. One should show how you brought people along. | ~50% |
| **Result** | Number, baseline and what it meant for the business | ~15% |
| **+ Learned** | One specific insight | ~8% |
| **+ Now I…** | The habit you've changed since | ~7% |

The whole story runs about 2 minutes (~280 words). Keep the actions to reasoning, not a list of tasks. Three steps with the "why" beat six steps without it.

Example of a strong "++" pair: "We hit the target, but I'd only set a floor for success. Now I agree a range with stakeholders in week one."

## Also write for each story

- **Headline** (≤ 14 words, includes the number). Example: "Cut onboarding drop-off 22% by redesigning KYC with risk and ops."
- **The line to land:** the one sentence they must not forget to say, usually the call they made. It gets highlighted on the one-pager.
- **30-second version.**
- **Follow-up prep:**
  - Decision log: the options, and why this one.
  - Numbers sheet: baseline, after, and how it was measured.
  - Who pushed back, and what they said.
  - One honest regret.
- **Likely probes** with one-line answers:
  - "What exactly did *you* do?"
  - "What else did you consider?"
  - "Who disagreed?"
  - "How did you measure it?"
  - "What would you do differently?"
  - "What happened after?"
- **Watch out for:** the one thing most likely to go wrong when they say it out loud.

## Check before showing the story

- [ ] It starts with the headline.
- [ ] More time goes on Action than on Situation.
- [ ] The call they made and what they gave up is explicit.
- [ ] It says "I" for their actions and "we" only for context.
- [ ] The result has a number, or an honest scope marker. Estimates are marked "~".
- [ ] No colleague is blamed.
- [ ] The learning is specific; "communication matters" doesn't count.
- [ ] No inflated words: leveraged, spearheaded, drove synergies, passionate.

Show the story, then ask: "What feels off, or not like you?" ⏸ Revise it, then move to the next story.
