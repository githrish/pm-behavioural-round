# Step 5: "Tell me about yourself"

This is the first question in almost every PM screen. It sets the tone, and the interviewer will ask about whatever you mention. Aakash Gupta interviewed senior PM candidates at Apollo.io and says 9 in 10 of them walked through their resume in order. Don't do that. The interviewer has already read the resume. What they want to know is whether you can tell a clear story about yourself that fits this role.

## What the interviewer is checking

- **Can you summarise?** If you can't explain your own career in 2 minutes, they'll doubt you can explain a product decision.
- **Does it fit?** They're asking whether your past points toward this role. They don't need your history.
- **Is there a logic?** Each move should make sense. Gaps and short stints shouldn't be left for them to guess about.
- **Do you want this?** Why product work, and why here.

## The outline: six beats, about 2 minutes

| # | Beat | What to say | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Headline** | One sentence: who you are as a professional and the thing you're best at. "I'm a ___ who ___." | 10 s |
| 2 | **Now** | Current role and scope, plus one result with a number. | 25 s |
| 3 | **How I got here** | 1 or 2 earlier moves told as one thread, not a timeline. Name the project you most want to be asked about, but don't tell it yet. That's your hook. | 40 s |
| 4 | **Why PM** *(switchers only)* | The moment you wanted to own what gets built, plus proof you've already done PM-type work. | 20 s |
| 5 | **Why here** | *Changes per company.* One thing about their product or problem that connects to your thread. | 15 s |
| 6 | **Handoff** | "Happy to go deeper on the ___ project." Then stop talking. | 5 s |

Existing PMs skip beat 4 and give the time to beat 3.

Write three lengths from the same beats:
- **30 seconds** (beats 1, 2, 6) for a recruiter who's short on time
- **2 minutes** (all beats), the default
- **Longer:** the 2-minute version plus one story told in full, for when they say "take your time"

## What makes it stand out

1. **Open with the headline, not your degree or your first job.** The first sentence is the one they remember and repeat in the debrief.
2. **One thread.** Pick the idea that connects your moves: "I keep ending up where customers and revenue meet." Every beat should support it.
3. **Plant hooks on purpose.** You decide what they ask next. Mention your strongest story by name in beat 3 and save the detail for later.
4. **One or two numbers.** Ten numbers is a resume. One well-chosen number is proof.
5. **Name the obvious doubt before they do.** If the resume shows no PM title, a gap or three jobs in four years, give it one honest line and turn it into a strength. Gupta's approach: find the 1 to 3 weaknesses a reviewer would spot and build your thread so each one reads as an asset.
6. **Show your real scope.** Say what you owned, not just what your team delivered. This matters most for people coming from IT services or agencies, where titles are often inflated.
7. **Stop at the handoff.** Ending cleanly signals confidence. Trailing off with "so yeah…" undercuts it.

## Same everywhere vs. changes per company

About 70 to 80% stays the same: beats 1 to 4 and the thread. Per company, change:
- **Beat 5** (why here). The role-tailoring skill writes this.
- **The order of proof in beat 3.** Lead with shipping for execution-heavy teams, user research for discovery teams, stakeholders and integrations for B2B or platform teams, and scrappiness for startups.
- **Wording.** Use the job description's words where they're accurate.

Don't put your full "why this company" answer into the intro. Give one line and let them ask.

## Common mistakes

- Walking through the resume in order
- Going past 2 minutes; most interviewers stop listening around then
- A generic ending ("I'm looking for new challenges")
- Leaving the interviewer to work out how your past connects to PM
- Memorising a script word for word. It sounds rehearsed and falls apart when interrupted. Memorise the beats instead.

## For people moving into PM

- Present the move as the next logical step, not a leap. Your thread should make PM sound inevitable.
- Put your strongest piece of PM-type work (discovery, a spec, an experiment, working with engineering) in beat 2 or 3, not at the end.
- Give "Why PM" a specific trigger moment, plus what you've done about it (side project, internal move, course).
- Make your background the angle:
  - **Sales/GTM:** closeness to customers and revenue
  - **Marketing/growth:** funnels and experiments
  - **Engineering:** technical depth and a pull toward the "why"
  - **Consulting:** structured problem-solving, and a wish to own outcomes instead of advising

## How to coach it

1. Draft the beats as **short phrases**, not a script. That's what goes on the one-pager.
2. Write the 2-minute version as spoken sentences for story-bank.md (~140 words per minute, so ~280 words).
3. Ask the user to say it in their own words and paste or dictate a transcript. Check it for:
   - the headline in the first sentence
   - one thread
   - the hook planted
   - the time
   - no resume walk
   - a clean handoff

   Give the single most important fix first.
